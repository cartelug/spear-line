What is in it

  • The full vehicle list as it is on the website today, with year, mileage, engine, fuel, exporter reference and price.
  • Markup. The website adds 8% to the exporter's price. A second option (the greater of 8% or USD 350) is shown beside it so the cheap cars still earn a fair margin.
  • Tax and landing costs. Draft rates by country and a worked Uganda example, with every unconfirmed figure marked.
  • A confirmation sheet listing the decisions needed.

How to read the prices

  • Prices are USD. They are exporter listings, not Spear Line stock. They were checked on 13 November 2026 and can be sold or changed at any time.
  • "Exporter FOB" is the vehicle itself. Freight, insurance, taxes, clearing, transport and registration are not included in the list prices.
  • "Spear Line price" is FOB plus the markup. It is what the website shows today, before import costs.
  • Everything marked TO CONFIRM or PLACEHOLDER is a draft figure that must be confirmed before it is shown to a customer.
To be updated. This list will be refreshed when exporter prices are rechecked, when tax rates are confirmed for each destination, and when the vehicles that sell are decided. Do not quote these totals to a customer.

Markup

Is 8% enough? Two options to choose from.

The website adds a flat 8% to the exporter's price. That is under USD 120 on a USD 1,500 car, which may not cover time, risk and the cost of a deal that falls through. Option B keeps 8% but never less than USD 350. Neither is confirmed: the owner sets the final markup.

Things the markup has to cover

  • Sourcing, inspection requests and follow-up with the exporter.
  • Money transfer and exchange-rate movement between order and payment.
  • A buyer who cancels after the exporter has reserved the car.
  • Claims about condition, mileage or paperwork found after arrival.

Questions for the owner

  • One rate for every car, or a higher rate on cheaper cars?
  • Is the markup the same for Uganda, Kenya, South Sudan and Congo buyers?
  • Is Spear Line paid in USD, and who carries exchange-rate risk?
